              2. The four marking criteria – what examiners look for

              Task Fulfillment~50%

              Content

              • Does it answer every point the prompt asks?
              • Right genre (email ≠ essay), enough words?
              • Does each point have a reason / example?

              → Underline every requirement; one requirement = one paragraph.

              Organization~10%

              Structure & cohesion

              • Opening – body – closing?
              • One main idea per paragraph?
              • Natural linking words, not repeated?

              → 4 paragraphs, 6–8 different linkers.

              Vocabulary~15%

              Vocabulary

              • Enough words for the topic?
              • Right context, right register (formal/informal)?
              • Little repetition, few spelling errors?

              → Paraphrase the prompt; use collocations you know well.

              Grammar~25%

              Grammar

              • Sentence variety: simple – compound – complex?
              • Correct tense, subject–verb agreement?
              • Errors that don't block the reader?

              → 1–2 reliable complex sentences per paragraph, the rest kept simple.

              5. 7 rules

              1

              Underline every requirement

              Missing one point costs Task Fulfillment marks immediately. Answer fully first, polish later.

              2

              Meet the word count

              Under the minimum loses marks. Target: 120–150 words / 260–300 words.

              3

              Match genre & register

              Emails have a greeting and sign-off; formal writing has no contractions; essays never say "Hi" or "I think it's cool".

              4

              One idea per paragraph

              The I – E – E formula: Idea → Explain → Example.

              5

              6–8 linkers, no repeats

              Firstly · In addition · However · For example · As a result · In conclusion

              6

              Write within your level

              One correct sentence beats three wrong ones. 1–2 reliable complex sentences per paragraph, the rest short.

              7

              Leave 3–5 minutes to proofread

              Tense – s/es – articles – spelling – punctuation – capital letters.

              1. What the prompt looks like

              Sample prompt

              You should spend about 20 minutes on this task. suggested time

              You have received an email from your English-speaking friend, Anna, who is planning to visit your city next month. Read part of her email: situation → who is the reader?

              3. Formal or informal?

              InformalFormal
              Writing toFriends, family, a pen pal (Anna, Ben, Jenny)A manager, a company, a school, a stranger, an organiser (Mr Lewis, Ms Wilson, Sir/Madam)
              GreetingDear Anna, / Hi Ben,Dear Mr Lewis, / Dear Ms Wilson, (name known) · Dear Sir or Madam, (name unknown)
              Sign-offBest wishes, / All the best, / Love, / Take care,Yours sincerely, (name known) · Yours faithfully, (Sir or Madam) · Kind regards,
              LanguageContractions (I'm, can't), short sentences, feelings, phrasal verbs, exclamation marksNo contractions, full sentences, indirect politeness (Could you… / I would be grateful if…), no slang
              Reason for writingThanks for your email. It was great to hear from you.Thank you for your email. I am writing to give you my feedback on…
              Semi-formal

              A former teacher, a host, a doctor, an older acquaintance → semi-formal: Dear Mrs Green, … Best regards,; polite, can be friendly, few contractions. When unsure: choose formal – it's the safer option.

              4. The 5-part frame

              1 · Greeting1 line
              Dear Anna, · Dear Mr Lewis, · Dear Sir or Madam,
              2 · Opening1–2 sentences
              Thanks / reason for writing: Thanks for your email. It was great to hear that… · Thank you for your email. I am writing to…
              3 · Body3 paragraphs × 2–3 sentences
              Point 1 First of all, … → answer + detail + reason
              Point 2 As for …, / Regarding …, / Secondly, …
              Point 3 Finally, … (don't skip point 3 just because time is short!)
              4 · Closing1–2 sentences
              Let me know if you need anything else. I can't wait to see you! · I look forward to hearing from you.
              5 · Sign-off1 line
              Best wishes, · Love, · Yours sincerely, · Yours faithfully, — no name

              All together ≈ 11–13 sentences ≈ 130–150 words.

              6. 7 common types

              Bold phrases are reusable – swap the details and reuse them for a different prompt.

              Type A · Replying to a friend – information & suggestions (informal)

              You have received an email from your English-speaking friend, Anna, who is planning to visit your city next month. Read part of her email:

              “I'm so excited about my trip! Could you help me with a few things? What's the weather like in your city in November? Which places do you think I should visit? And where would be a good, not too expensive place to stay?”

              Write an email of reply to Anna, and:

              • tell her about the weather in November,
              • suggest two places she should visit, and
              • recommend a place to stay.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model A · informal… words

              Dear Anna,

              Thanks for your email. It was great to hear that you are coming to my city next month. Here is some information that I hope will help.

              First of all, the weather in November is quite pleasant. It is usually cool and dry, so you will not need heavy clothes, but I suggest bringing a light jacket for the evenings.

              As for places to visit, you should definitely see the old town, where you can try local street food, and the Central Museum, which shows the history of the region.

              Finally, I'd recommend staying at the Riverside Guesthouse. It is clean, friendly and only ten minutes from my house, and a room costs about twenty dollars a night.

              Let me know when you arrive so that I can pick you up at the airport. I can't wait to see you!

              Best wishes,

              Why it scores well: all 3 points covered, each one = answer + detail (cool and dry · twenty dollars a night) + reason; consistently informal (I'd, can't, Best wishes); 2 relative clauses (where…, which…) are enough to reach B2.
              Type B · Feedback – suggestions (formal)

              You recently attended a two-day workshop on communication skills for health workers, organised by the training centre CareTalk. After the event, you received this email from the coordinator, Mr Lewis:

              “Thank you for taking part in our workshop. To help us improve, could you tell us which part of the workshop you found most useful, mention one thing we could do better, and let us know whether you would join another CareTalk event in the future?”

              Write an email of reply to Mr Lewis, and:

              • tell him which part you found most useful,
              • suggest one thing that could be improved, and
              • say whether you would join another event and why.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model B · formal… words

              Dear Mr Lewis,

              Thank you for your email. I am writing to give you my feedback on the communication skills workshop, which I attended last week.

              The part I found most useful was the role-play session on the second day. Practising difficult conversations with "patients" helped me feel more confident, and the trainer's comments were clear and practical.

              However, one thing that could be improved is the timetable. The morning lectures were rather long, and there was little time for questions. It would be better if each lecture were shorter and followed by a short discussion.

              Overall, I was very satisfied with the workshop, so I would certainly attend another CareTalk event, especially one on working with patients' families.

              Thank you once again for a well-organised course. I look forward to hearing about future events.

              Yours sincerely,

              Why it scores well: correctly formal (no contractions, Dear Mr Lewis → Yours sincerely); the suggestion is polite (It would be better if…); point 3 gives a reason. This frame works for any "feedback on a course / conference / hotel / service" prompt.
              Type C · Invitation (informal)

              You are organising a small party to celebrate finishing your course. Write an email to your friend Ben, and:

              • invite him to the party and say when and where it is,
              • tell him what you are planning to do at the party, and
              • ask him to help you with something.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model C · informal… words

              Hi Ben,

              How are things? I hope you're well. I'm writing to invite you to a small party to celebrate the end of my course. It will be held at my flat on Saturday 25 October at 7 p.m.

              We're planning to have a barbecue in the garden, so there will be plenty of food and cold drinks. Later on, we'll play some board games and, if the weather is good, watch a film outside. It's going to be very relaxed, so just wear something comfortable.

              There's one thing I'd like to ask you. Could you bring your speaker and help me choose the music? You have much better taste than I do!

              Let me know if you can come. It would be great to see you there.

              Best wishes,

              Why it scores well: clear time and place (at … on … at …); plans use be going to / will; a polite request with Could you…?; a first conditional (if the weather is good).
              Type D · Apology + alternative arrangement (informal)

              Your English friend, Jenny, is planning to visit you next month, but you cannot meet her on the dates she suggested. Write an email to Jenny, and:

              • apologise for not being able to meet her,
              • explain the reason, and
              • suggest a different time to meet.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model D · informal… words

              Dear Jenny,

              Thanks for your message. It's lovely to hear that you're coming to Vietnam next month.

              I'm really sorry, but I'm afraid I won't be able to meet you on 12–14 May, as you suggested. The reason is that I have my final exams that week, and I need to spend every free moment studying. I feel terrible about this, because I know you've already planned your trip.

              However, how about meeting on the following weekend, 19–20 May? By then my exams will be over, and I'll be completely free. We could spend Saturday exploring the old quarter and take a boat trip on Sunday. If that doesn't suit you, just tell me which days work best and I'll arrange my time.

              Once again, I'm sorry for the change of plan. I look forward to seeing you soon.

              Love,

              Why it scores well: apology – reason – new suggestion flow in a clear sequence; uses a future perfect-style form (will be over) and a conditional; closing with another apology feels genuinely human.
              Type E · Requesting information (formal)

              You saw an advertisement for a summer English course at Bright Language School in Manchester. Write an email to the school, and:

              • say which course you are interested in and ask when it starts and how much it costs,
              • ask about accommodation, and
              • ask whether you will receive a certificate at the end.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model E · formal… words

              Dear Sir or Madam,

              I am writing to ask for some information about the four-week summer English course advertised on your website.

              First of all, I would like to know when the course starts and how much the fee is. Could you also tell me whether the price includes course books and materials?

              Secondly, I would be grateful if you could give me some details about accommodation. Is it possible to stay with a local family, and if so, how much does it cost per week?

              Finally, I would also like to ask whether students receive a certificate at the end of the course, as I need one for my job application.

              I look forward to your reply. Thank you in advance for your help.

              Yours faithfully,

              Why it scores well: 3 polite indirect questions (I would like to know when… / whether…) – correct B2 grammar; Dear Sir or Madam → Yours faithfully is the correct pair.
              Type F · Complaint – request (formal)

              You recently bought a laptop from an online shop, but there were several problems. Write an email to the customer service department, and:

              • say what you bought and when,
              • explain the problems, and
              • say what you want the shop to do.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model F · formal… words

              Dear Sir or Madam,

              I am writing to complain about a laptop (order number 4521) that I bought from your online shop on 3 March.

              Unfortunately, there were several problems with the order. First, the laptop arrived five days later than promised. In addition, when I opened the box, I found that the screen was scratched and the charger was missing. As a result, I have not been able to use the computer for my work.

              I would therefore like to request a replacement or a full refund. I would also be grateful if you could explain why the order was delayed.

              I hope this matter can be resolved as soon as possible. I look forward to hearing from you.

              Yours faithfully,

              Why it scores well: complains politely, with detail (order number, date); the consequence is signalled with As a result; the request is clear (replacement / refund).
              Type G · Thank-you letter (informal)

              Last month you stayed with your friend Lisa's family in England. Write an email to Lisa, and:

              • thank her and her family for the stay,
              • say what you enjoyed most, and
              • invite her to visit you.

              Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses.

              Model G · informal… words

              Dear Lisa,

              I'm writing to thank you and your family for letting me stay with you last month. It was so kind of you to look after me so well.

              What I enjoyed most was the weekend trip to the Lake District. The views were amazing, and I still laugh when I remember how we got lost on the way back! I also loved your mum's cooking – please tell her that her apple pie was the best I've ever had.

              To show my appreciation, I'd love you to come and visit me next summer. You could stay with my family for a week, and I'll take you to the beach and to the night market. I'm sure you'd enjoy it.

              Thanks again for everything. Hope to hear from you soon!

              Love,

              Why it scores well: thanks for specific things (the trip, the food), not generic thanks; a superlative + present perfect (the best I've ever had); the invitation includes a plan.

              3. Five essay types – how to spot them & structure them

              1Opinion

              Spot it: To what extent do you agree or disagree? · Do you agree…? · Which opinion do you agree with?
              1. Intro: paraphrase + a clear stance (agree / disagree)
              2. Body 1: reason 1 + explanation + example
              3. Body 2: reason 2 + explanation + example
              4. (Optional 1–2 counter-argument sentences: Some may argue… However…)
              5. Conclusion: restate your stance + sum up the 2 reasons

              2Discussion

              Spot it: Discuss both views and give your opinion · Some people think… while others…
              1. Intro: state both sides + say you'll discuss both
              2. Body 1: side A – reason – example
              3. Body 2: side B – reason – example
              4. Conclusion: your own opinion (which side you lean towards)

              3Advantages / Disadvantages

              Spot it: advantages and disadvantages · positive and negative effects · benefits and drawbacks
              1. Intro: context + say you'll cover both sides
              2. Body 1: 2 advantages (+ example)
              3. Body 2: 2 disadvantages (+ example)
              4. Conclusion: which side is stronger (outweigh)

              4Problem / Solution

              Spot it: What problems…? How can they be solved? · causes… solutions · What can be done…?
              1. Intro: state the problem + say you'll cover causes/problems and solutions
              2. Body 1: 2 problems / causes
              3. Body 2: 2–3 solutions matched to the problems above (who does what)
              4. Conclusion: sum up + the condition for solving it

              5Two-part question

              Spot it: the prompt has 2 different questions: What effects…? What can parents do…?
              1. Intro: context + say you'll answer both
              2. Body 1: answer question 1
              3. Body 2: answer question 2
              4. Conclusion: sum up both answers
              Choosing a side

              You don't have to write what you actually believe. Pick whichever side you can back up with 2 reasons + 2 examples within a minute. Keep that side from the introduction to the conclusion – switching stance halfway costs marks in both Task Fulfillment and Organization.

              4. The 4-paragraph frame = 13 sentences ≈ 260 words

              Introduction2 sentences · ~40 words
              ① Paraphrase the prompt – context (In recent years, … has become …)
              ② Answer the prompt / say what the essay will do (I completely agree that… · This essay will discuss both views…)
              Body 15 sentences · ~90 words
              ① Topic sentence = point 1 (The first reason is that…) ② Explanation (This is because… / This means that…) ③ Example (For example, in Vietnam…) ④ Result / extension (As a result, …) ⑤ Closing sentence (In other words, …)
              Body 25 sentences · ~90 words
              Same formula: point 2 → explanation → example → result → closing sentence. Open with a different linker from Body 1 (Another important reason is that… / On the other hand, …)
              Conclusion1–2 sentences · ~40 words
              ① In conclusion, + restate the 2 points in different words ② Confirm your stance / give a recommendation (Therefore, I believe that…). No new ideas.
              I – E – E

              Each body paragraph only needs Idea → Explain → Example. A simple example is enough: In Vietnam, for example, many office workers now complain of back pain. It doesn't need to be exact – it needs to be plausible.

              7. Upgrading a B1 sentence to B2: 8 reliable ways

              Left: correct but plain (B1). Right: same idea, one more structure – B2 level. Each body paragraph only needs 1–2 sentences like this.

              Relative clauseFast food is bad for health. It has a lot of fat.
              →
              Fast food, which is high in fat and salt, can seriously damage our health.
              Although + tend toMany people use smartphones. They use them too much.
              →
              Although smartphones are useful, many people tend to use them far more than necessary.
              Second conditionalThe government should build more parks. People will do exercise.
              →
              If the government built more parks, people would be more likely to exercise regularly.
              Not only … but alsoOnline shopping is convenient. It is cheap.
              →
              Online shopping is not only convenient but also cheaper than traditional shopping.
              It is widely believed thatPeople think that money is important.
              →
              It is widely believed that money plays an important role in a happy life.
              Passive + by V-ingWe can reduce pollution. We use public transport.
              →
              Pollution can be reduced by using public transport instead of private cars.
              The more …, the more …Children play games a lot. They get fat.
              →
              The more time children spend on games, the higher the risk of obesity becomes.
              There is no doubt that + whichThis is a big problem. We must solve it.
              →
              There is no doubt that this is a serious problem which needs to be addressed urgently.

              9. Five model essays – one per type

              Bold phrases are reusable. The orange label marks its place in the 4-paragraph frame. Written at a comfortable B2 level – no sentence over 30 words.

              Type 1 · Opinion (agree / disagree)

              You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Read the following topic:

              Some people think that health education should be a compulsory subject in all secondary schools. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

              Write an essay to express your point of view. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience.

              Write at least 250 words.

              Model 1 · Opinion… words

              It is often argued that health education should be a compulsory subject in secondary schools. I completely agree with this view, because healthy habits are formed at a young age and many families do not have enough knowledge to teach them.

              The first reason is that teenagers make many decisions that affect their health for the rest of their lives. For example, young people start eating fast food, using smartphones late at night or even smoking during their school years. If they learn about balanced diets, sleep and the dangers of tobacco in class, they are more likely to make better choices. In other words, school is the best place to build good habits before bad ones become fixed.

              Another important reason is that not all parents can give their children correct information. In many families, especially in rural areas, parents work long hours and have little knowledge of nutrition or mental health. As a result, teenagers often get advice from the internet, which is not always reliable. Even worse, some websites sell dangerous diets or fake medicines to young people. A compulsory subject taught by trained teachers would ensure that every student, regardless of family background, receives the same basic knowledge.

              Some people may argue that the school timetable is already full. However, one or two lessons a week would be enough, and the long-term benefits clearly outweigh this small cost.

              In conclusion, I strongly believe that health education should be compulsory in secondary schools, since it helps young people form healthy habits and gives all students equal access to reliable information.

              Why it scores well: a clear stance from sentence 2, restated in the conclusion; 2 reasons, each with an example; a short counter-argument paragraph shows balanced thinking (a B2 plus point); varied complex sentences: if…, which…, regardless of….
              Type 2 · Discussion (both views + opinion)

              You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Read the following topic:

              Some people think that fast food should be avoided because of the health problems it may cause, while others believe that it is a good choice for people with a busy life. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

              Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience.

              Write at least 250 words.

              Model 2 · Discussion… words

              People have different views about fast food. While some believe it should be avoided because it damages our health, others see it as a convenient choice for busy people. This essay will discuss both views before giving my own opinion.

              On the one hand, there are good reasons to limit fast food. It is well known that burgers, fried chicken and soft drinks contain high levels of fat, salt and sugar. If people eat them regularly, they may put on weight and develop serious conditions such as diabetes and heart disease. In Vietnam, for instance, the number of overweight children in big cities has risen sharply in recent years, and doctors often blame fast-food habits. Clearly, people who care about their long-term health have good reasons to avoid it.

              On the other hand, supporters of fast food point out that it saves time and money. Many office workers and students have less than thirty minutes for lunch, and a fast-food meal is cheap, quick and available everywhere. Without this option, many of them would skip lunch completely, which is even worse for their health. In addition, most restaurants now offer healthier options, such as salads and grilled chicken, so customers can make better choices if they want to.

              In my opinion, fast food is acceptable as long as it is eaten occasionally and not every day, and is combined with fresh food cooked at home. In conclusion, although fast food is convenient for people with busy lives, I believe that its health risks mean it should only be a small part of our diet.

              Why it scores well: both sides are balanced, each with a reason + example; the writer's own opinion appears only in the conclusion – matching "discuss both views and give your opinion"; 8 different linkers, none repeated.
              Type 3 · Advantages – Disadvantages

              You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Read the following topic:

              These days, more and more people prefer to shop online rather than in traditional shops. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend?

              Write an essay to express your point of view. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience.

              Write at least 250 words.

              Model 3 · Adv – Disadv… words

              In recent years, online shopping has become increasingly popular, and many people now buy everything from food to furniture on the internet. This trend has both advantages and disadvantages, which will be discussed in this essay.

              The main advantage of shopping online is convenience. Customers can order products at any time and from anywhere, without travelling to a shop or waiting in a queue. This is particularly useful for people who live far from big cities or who work long hours. Another benefit is that prices are often lower, because online stores do not have to pay for expensive shops and staff. For example, a textbook that costs 200,000 dong in a bookshop can often be found online for 150,000 dong, delivered to your door.

              However, there are also some drawbacks. The biggest disadvantage is that buyers cannot see or try the product before paying. As a result, clothes may not fit, and the quality is sometimes much lower than in the photos. Moreover, online shopping can be addictive: since it is so easy to click "buy", people often spend more than they planned. There is also the risk of fake websites that steal customers' money or personal information. In Vietnam, for example, newspapers regularly report cases of shoppers who paid for goods that never arrived.

              In conclusion, online shopping offers convenience and lower prices, but it also brings problems of quality and security. On balance, I believe that the advantages outweigh the disadvantages, provided that customers buy from trusted websites and think carefully before they pay, rather than buying on impulse.

              Why it scores well: each body paragraph has 2 points + 1 concrete example (the textbook price); the conclusion weighs both sides with outweigh … provided that; the opening and closing phrases are reusable for any advantages–disadvantages prompt.
              Type 4 · Problem – Solution

              You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Read the following topic:

              More and more people are moving from the countryside to live in cities. What problems can this cause? How can these problems be solved?

              Write an essay to express your point of view. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience.

              Write at least 250 words.

              Model 4 · Problem – Solution… words

              Nowadays, a large number of people are leaving rural areas to look for a better life in cities. This migration causes several serious problems, but there are also practical solutions.

              The most obvious problem is overcrowding. When too many people arrive in a short time, cities cannot provide enough housing, so newcomers often live in small, poor-quality rooms. This leads to heavy traffic, air pollution and long waits at hospitals and schools. Another problem is unemployment: because many migrants have few qualifications, they can only find low-paid or temporary jobs, and some end up in poverty. In many cases, they also have no health insurance, so a single illness can destroy a family's savings.

              Fortunately, there are several ways to deal with these problems. First of all, governments should invest in rural areas by building factories, roads and good schools there, so that people do not need to move away to find work. In Vietnam, for example, new industrial zones in provinces such as Bac Ninh have created thousands of jobs close to home. Secondly, city authorities could build more affordable housing and improve public transport to reduce traffic and pollution. Finally, free training courses would help migrants gain the skills they need for better jobs. At the same time, employers should be encouraged to offer fair wages and proper contracts to workers from the countryside.

              In conclusion, migration from the countryside to cities leads to overcrowding and unemployment. However, if governments develop rural areas and cities plan carefully for new residents, these problems can be greatly reduced, and both cities and villages will benefit.

              Why it scores well: each solution is matched to a specific problem (lack of jobs ↔ rural investment; overcrowding ↔ housing + transport); every solution names who acts (governments / city authorities); the Vietnam example is plausible, with no need for exact figures.
              Type 5 · Two-part question

              You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Read the following topic:

              Many children today spend hours playing computer games. What effects does this have on children? What can parents do to reduce the negative effects?

              Write an essay to express your point of view. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience.

              Write at least 250 words.

              Model 5 · Two-part… words

              Computer games have become one of the most popular activities for children around the world. This essay will examine the effects of this habit and suggest what parents can do to limit its negative side.

              Playing computer games has both positive and negative effects. On the positive side, many games improve children's problem-solving skills and quick thinking, and online games help them make friends with players from other countries. However, the negative effects are more serious. Children who play for hours every day tend to do less exercise and sleep less, which can lead to obesity and poor concentration at school. Furthermore, some games contain violence, and young children may copy aggressive behaviour they see on the screen. Over time, this can also damage family relationships, because children who spend all evening in front of a screen rarely talk to their parents.

              There are several things that parents can do. The first step is to set clear rules about screen time – for example, no more than one hour on school days, and no games before homework is finished. Secondly, parents should check the age rating of each game and avoid those with violent content. Most importantly, parents need to offer attractive alternatives, such as sports, music or family trips, so that children do not feel that gaming is their only source of fun.

              In conclusion, computer games can develop some useful skills, but too much gaming harms children's health and behaviour. By setting limits and providing other activities, parents can make sure that games remain a small and healthy part of their children's lives.

              Why it scores well: one paragraph per question, answering both in full; paragraph 1 covers both positive and negative effects, then leans negative – setting up question 2; the solutions are specific and measurable (no more than one hour).

              • Firstly · In addition · Moreover · Furthermore
              • For example · For instance · such as
              • However · On the other hand · Although · Nevertheless
              • because · since · As a result · Therefore · This leads to

              Last 5-minute check
              • Enough words? T1 ≥ 120 (≥ 14 lines) · T2 ≥ 250 (≥ 28 lines)
              • All points covered? T1: all 3 bullets · T2: every question in the prompt
              • Greeting – sign-off match the register, no name included
              • Tense · s/es · a/an/the · spelling · capital letters
              • Formal & essays: no contractions (don't → do not)
